# GBS Release Notes
Unreleased

Features
* `gbs-io/brep_json.h` (new): exact files of a BREP model (`docs/sources/design/io_brep_json.md`, which also specifies the format and answers the requirements of CadSmith's review, need G11). `write_brep(m)` (a string; a stream; `write_brep_file`, whole or not at all; `write_brep_value`, a value of an application's own JSON document) and `read_brep<T>(text)` (a stream, `read_brep_file`, `read_brep_value`) give back the model that was written, not a copy of its shapes: every table at the same size, the dead entities included (dead again once read), so that every id held outside the model designates the same entity without renumbering; `write_brep_compact` writes it as `Model::compact()` makes it instead, with that `IdRemap`, the model untouched. The ids stored in the entities as they are, orientations, tolerances, flags, names, external ids, instances of parts, the next part key, the unit. The geometry is written once per object (by address) and referred to by index: shared by several entities or nested in another geometry, it is shared again once read (the same graph, not a tree). Every real bit for bit (shortest decimal form, read in full precision; -0 and subnormals included). The classes held are recognized by their exact type: B-spline and NURBS curves and surfaces (2D, 3D; their bounds when they differ from the end knots), line, ellipse, plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus (their frames and boxes as they are), curves trimmed, composite, reversed, reparametrized, extended, on a surface, offset, moved, surfaces of revolution, offset, moved and seamless; the result of every operation of gbs-brep is checked to round trip. Errors are `BuildResult`s, never exceptions: writing refuses, naming the entity and before anything is written, another class (the old `Line` and `Circle`, whose constructors normalize, a class of an application), a number not finite or a name not UTF-8 (the text is standard JSON) with `UnsupportedEntity`, an id beyond its table with `InvalidId`; reading refuses a newer version with `UnsupportedVersion` (new), and a text cut short, not a model or corrupt with `InvalidFile`, the message giving the JSON pointer of the value at fault: parsed without recursion, a strict schema (an unknown or repeated key is a fault), every index below its table, a geometry referring only to those before it and of the right kind, the knots of B-splines checked, the refusals of gbs's constructors reported at the geometry (tested on 3,000 randomly damaged texts, under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer). Writing is a pure function of the const model, deterministic (the same model, the same bytes). JSON (RapidJSON) rather than binary, on measurements: 0.6 to 0.9 times the size of the STEP file, read and written at 0.35 to 0.55 GB/s (a part of 18.8 MB of STEP read in 9.6 s: 10.7 MB of JSON read in 24 ms; 30,000 faces: 16.6 MB written in 36 ms, read in 44 ms). `gbs-brep/equal.h` (new): `brep::equal(a, b)` and `brep::first_difference(a, b)` ("edge 12: B-spline poles differ"): the same tables and ids, fields, attributes, geometry bit for bit (defining data and values at samples) and sharing. `gbs-brep/seamless.h` (new): `SeamlessSurface`, the view past its seam of a closed B-spline face that the contacts of a fillet keep in the model, moved out of `fillet.h` so that the writer knows it. `Model::nextPart()`, `Model::reservePartKeys(next)`; `BuildErrc::UnsupportedVersion`; `unwrap` of a `BuildResult<void>`. Python: `brep.write_brep(model, path, pretty=False)`, `brep.write_brep_text`, `brep.write_brep_compact_text` (text, remap), `brep.read_brep(path)`, `brep.read_brep_text`, `brep.equal`, `brep.first_difference`, a failure raising `BRepError` with its code; a `Model` pickles through the same text.
* `gbs-brep/face_blend.h`, `gbs-brep/blend.h`, `gbs-brep/fillet.h`, `gbs-brep/radius_law.h`: partial strips, laws and sections of fillets between faces (stage 8, PR 2, `docs/sources/design/brep8_pr02_partial_strips_laws.md`). `fillet_faces` takes faces that already meet along part of their length, a strip running on from their common edge e elsewhere: a face G of neither side that touches both, its edges with them ending on a vertex of e, is of the strip, not an end face (a face tapering to a point on e, the triangle closing a chamfer stopped on e, a chamfer between two common edges); on the copy the virtual edge continues e, both fused into one edge (the intersection recomputed over their union, the vertex between them taken out), one fillet in one piece, as that of the edge before the strip (to 1e-9 in volume, and against `BRepFilletAPI_MakeFillet`; PR 1 refused it: "meet along edges in places only"); a radius too small spills over the strip, refused (a face 0.085 wide from the corner of a U: refused at r = 0.2, the arc 0.083 from it, swallowed at 0.3). `fillet` of the edge alone still does not cross the strip, its refusal at the end naming `fillet_faces` ("faces between its faces there, fillet_faces swallows them"). `fillet_faces(m, s, faces1, faces2, RadiusLaw, options)`, `fillet_faces_chordal(m, s, faces1, faces2, c, options)`, and the sections of stage 7 (conic, G2) on the virtual edge. `BlendPoint::at_point(p, value)`: a point of a law given by position, put at its foot on the nearest edge blended (on the virtual edge for a fillet of faces, its points on edges of the solid put there too), for every law of fillets and chamfers, the geometry of one edge too (`fillet_geometry`, `chamfer_geometry`, a conic section, a law of one value included); refused (`InvalidBounds`) farther than ten times its value and the length of the edge, or at the same foot as another of a different value. A common edge from the strip to the strip (the rest of the foot of a boss chamfered over its seam) lies under the virtual edge from end to end; strips on both sides of a common edge are refused; of several strips tried, the refusal given is that of the one closed over. `fillet_faces_chordal` shares the geometry of `fillet_chordal` (`blend_detail::chordal_blend`). Python: `brep.fillet_faces(..., law, options)`, `brep.fillet_faces_chordal`, `brep.BlendPoint.at_point(point, value)`, the rule of the faces swallowed in the docstring of `fillet_faces`; gallery: `fillet_faces_tapered`.
* `gbs-brep/face_blend.h` (new): fillets between two faces that have no edge in common (stage 8, PR 1, `docs/sources/design/brep_palier8_face_blends.md`, `docs/sources/design/brep8_pr01_face_blends.md`). `fillet_faces(m, s, faces1, faces2, r, options)`: each side a face or faces joined tangentially (the side of a stadium slab); the faces between them (the strip, bounded by end faces that touch both sides) swallowed. On a copy of the solid the strip is taken out and both sides continued until they meet: the edges ending on the strip (spokes: between a side and an end face, two faces of a side, a seam) continued to the face across, the virtual edge between those points on the intersection of the sides continued, the wires of the faces kept closed over it; the fillet of that edge (`fillet`: free ends on the end faces, chains, sections), kept if the strip lies wholly on the side it takes off, or adds (a groove filled in: its edges with the end faces then on the result). Faces that all have edges in common with the other side (and no strip elsewhere): the fillet of those edges. Refused: a fillet that spills over the strip (a radius too small), sides that do not meet once continued (parallel faces, two ribs on a plate), faces that meet along edges in places only (a chamfer along part of an edge), a strip that runs round the rest of the solid (a chamfer ending on another chamfer: no end face touches both sides); `InvalidId`: a face on both sides, the faces of a side not joined tangentially (within `angular_tol`). Against the fillet of the edge before the chamfer: the same solid, volumes to 1e-9 (box, step, V groove), 1e-8 (the foot of a boss, a torus), 1e-7 (the foot of a stadium slab, a set of four faces); OCCT's `BRepFilletAPI_MakeFillet` on that edge: the same solid. Python: `brep.fillet_faces`. Two gallery cases.
* `gbs-brep/blend.h`: fillets that swallow a pocket (`docs/sources/design/brep_swallowed_faces.md`, `docs/sources/design/brep_swallowed_faces_pr01.md`). Where the two faces of filleted edges meet again past the ends of the edges round a few small faces (the sliver left on the top of a cylinder cut by a tilted plane, a shallow flat across an edge), and the fillet passes beside them without touching them, `fillet` (radius or law) and `fillet_chordal` fill the pocket on a copy (the surfaces of both faces continued, their intersection from one end to the other as an edge, the bridge), fillets the chain through the bridge, and keeps the result when every point of the pocket is on its side of the result and no face of the pocket meets it: the pocket and its edges, given or not, disappear, with the points of a law and the setbacks on them (convex and concave edges). A bridge on a circle or an ellipse may run across the origin of its parameter. A fillet that cuts through a pocket instead (it rolls over a sharp edge next to it) is refused as `overflow` where the mitres and free ends refuse it too. At a joint of a chain, the arc of the second fillet is projected from that of the first when both surfaces put it on other parameters (it was refused). Not handled: chamfers (next PR), a seam of either face running into the pocket, the two ends of one edge bounding a pocket.
* `gbs/movingframe.h` (new): `MovingFrame`, a moving frame along a curve (o on it, z the unit tangent): rotation-minimizing (`FrameLaw::RotationMinimizing`, the double reflection of Wang, Jüttler, Zheng and Liu, the stations doubled until the frame moves by less than `angle_tol`, one double reflection from the previous station in between; on a closed path the residual angle, `closure_angle()`, spread along it), Frenet (refused where the curvature vanishes, where its binormal reverses, also between the samples or at a break, and on a curve without second derivatives) or a fixed binormal (refused where the tangent is along it, also between the samples); breaks of a path only G1 are stations, the frame at the end of the piece before a break is the limit from the left; an `angle_tol` that cannot be reached is a `std::runtime_error`. `gbs-brep/pipe.h` (new): `make_pipe(model, profile, path, PipeOptions)` (stage 4, PR 2, `docs/sources/design/brep4_pr02_pipe.md`), the sweep of an edge, a wire or a face along an edge or a G1 wire, carried by the frame (`PipeOptions::frame`, `binormal`), the profile taken as it is: along a segment where the frame does not turn, the prism of the placed profile, along an arc of circle (recognized on its points, whatever the type of its curve) where it turns with the arc, its revolution (planes, cylinders, cones, spheres, tori, exact NURBS), each path edge swept by its own frame up to its end; otherwise B-spline faces, the profile placed at stations and skinned, the stations doubled until within `approx_tol` of the exact sweep (`SweepResult::approximated`, `deviation`); a face gives a solid closed by the profile and its copy at the end, no caps along a closed path. Refused (`NonGeneric`): a corner of the path (its angle times the size of the profile beyond the tolerance of the junction, `tol` or that of its vertex), a frame that moves the profile at a junction beyond it (Frenet across a change of the principal normal), a profile beyond the radius of curvature of the path (the surface folds), a sweep that intersects itself away from the folds (the path coming back within the size of the profile), a frame undefined on the path, a closed path along which the profile does not come back onto itself; a profile that is not an edge, a wire or a face is `InvalidId`, tolerances that are not positive `InvalidBounds`, an accuracy that cannot be reached `PCurveApproximation`; the model is left as it was. `sweep_detail::sweep` sews within a given tolerance at least (the gaps at the junctions of a path). `gbs-brep/edit.h`: `moved_curve(curve, placement)`. Python: `brep.make_pipe`, `brep.PipeOptions`, `brep.PipeFrame`. Gallery: a section of sweeps; the OpenCascade comparison gains `pipes` (`BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell`)

* `gbs-brep/thick.h` (new): `offset_solid(model, solid, d, OffsetOptions)` and `make_thick(model, solid, removed, t, OffsetOptions)` (stage 4, PR 4, `docs/sources/design/brep4_pr04_offset_solid.md`). The offset of a solid with sharp joints, each face on its offset surface (exact for the primitives) along the outward normal (d > 0 grows it): an edge where the offsets of its faces meet along one curve (tangent faces at the same distance, a seam) is its pcurve on the offset, continued past its ends as needed; any other edge is the intersection of the offsets of its two faces (closed forms between primitives, marching otherwise), the branch through its new vertices (on a loop, the arc nearest the old edge; a closed edge, the loop run from its vertex, also where the box of a closed B-spline cuts it at its seam); the faces are continued past their sharp edges by what the joint needs (a primitive on a wider box, a B-spline by the polynomials of its end spans, but across its seam or past a pole); a vertex is the point common to the offsets around it (Gauss-Newton started off the poles, its steps where a curve cannot be evaluated halved: on its tangent edge when it has one, on all its faces otherwise, between two faces, a vertex between two arcs of a circle, in the plane across it), a pole of a face at a sharp vertex left out of its wire. Solids written by other systems are offset: circles in two arcs, vertices at the poles of a sphere, cylinders of NURBS (where OpenCascade's own offset fails). A thick solid is the solid minus its offset by -t with the removed faces offset by 0 (`cut`, the coincident faces opening it); t < 0 grows outward; no removed face gives a hollow solid with a void. Refused (`NonGeneric`): an offset that folds (the apex of a cone), a face that vanishes, an offset intersecting itself (faces sharing no vertex that meet), a vertex where the offsets do not meet at one point, a face whose offset would cover the pole of its sphere at a sharp vertex (a ball cut through its poles, offset outward), an edge partly tangent, a joint too sharp to be continued (`max_extension`); the model is left as it was. `gbs/bsctools.h`: `extrapolated(crv, dt)`, a B-spline curve continued by the polynomials of its end spans (the curve counterpart of `extrapolated_v`); the internal namespaces of `gbs/bsstools.h` renamed (`bss_detail`, `bsc_detail`), so that `gbs-brep/brep` does not make `detail::` ambiguous under `using namespace gbs; using namespace gbs::brep;`. Python: `brep.offset_solid`, `brep.make_thick`, `brep.OffsetOptions`. Gallery: a section of offset and thick solids; the OpenCascade comparison gains `offsets` (`BRepOffsetAPI_MakeOffsetShape`, `BRepOffsetAPI_MakeThickSolid`, sharp joints: same volumes and faces; on solids OpenCascade cannot offset, gbs's result read back valid, with its exact volume)
* `gbs-brep/fillet.h`: the G2 section of fillets (stage 7, PR 3, `docs/sources/design/brep7_pr03_g2_sections.md`). `BlendSection::g2()` (`SectionKind::G2`): the quartic Bezier tangent to the faces with their curvature across at its contacts, of the smoothest shape (the least integral of the square of the derivative of its curvature), its contacts spread for the sag of the arc of radius r (1.305 r at right angles, rule C of `docs/sources/design/brep_palier7_blend_sections.md`, § 4.4). Refused: with a constant chord, with a radius law that kinks. Python: `brep.SectionKind.G2`, `brep.BlendSection.g2()`. The summary of stage 7 in its design note, § 9.
* `gbs-brep/fillet.h`, `gbs-brep/blend.h`: sections of fillets (stage 7, PR 2, `docs/sources/design/brep7_pr02_conic_chordal.md`). `FilletOptions::section` (`BlendSection`: `circular()`, `conic(rho)`, `conic(law)`): the conic tangent to the faces at the contacts of the fillet of the radius, the middle pole of its arc of circle weighted rho / (1 - rho) (rho of the circle: the circle; 0.5 a parabola), rho constant or following a `BlendLaw`; exact extrusions (rational, degree (2, 1)) along a straight centre line, skinned elsewhere, the normal checked against the exact sections; where a law of rho kinks the surface creases (6.5 degrees for 0.3, 0.7, 0.3), a fillet of constant radius in pieces there sharing the section, refused along a radius law. `fillet_chordal(m, s, edges, c)`, `fillet_chordal_geometry`: the contacts c apart at every station, the radius following the angle of the faces (secant on the radius, the balls marched from the middle of the edge, as far as the fillet is continued: along chains too), the envelope of the balls of a radius law. * `gbs-brep/fillet.h` (new): the geometry of a constant-radius fillet and of a chamfer along an edge of a solid, the model left unchanged (stage 4, PR 5, `docs/sources/design/brep4_pr05_fillet_geometry.md`): `fillet_geometry(model, solid, edge, r, FilletOptions)` and `chamfer_geometry(model, solid, edge, face, d1, d2, FilletOptions)` (d1 on `face`) return an `EdgeBlend` (the surface, exact or within `deviation`, the centre line or the edge continued, the two contacts as `BlendContact`: the face, its surface continued, the pcurve on it, the curve, the pcurve on the blend, every curve on the same parameter, `t1` and `t2` at the vertices of the edge, `t1 > t2` where the spine runs the other way, a closed edge included). The ball lies inside a convex edge and outside a concave one (read on the outward normals and the directions of the faces away from the edge); its centre line is the intersection of the surfaces of the faces, continued and offset by r toward it (closed forms between primitives, marching otherwise), the branch nearest the edge, continued by `extension` r past its vertices (a closed edge: its whole loop); the contacts are its feet on the faces. A chamfer takes its distances from the edge in the plane across it (as CATIA, SolidWorks and OpenCascade), each contact marched from the middle of the edge with a first-order predictor, on the side of the face, and interpolated within tol / 10 (the accuracy of the contacts of a fillet on primitives); the edge is continued past its vertices whatever its curve (a curve on a surface, the edges of a boolean, by its pcurve on the surface of its face continued); the ruled surface between the contacts is a plane, a cylinder or a cone when they are lines in a plane or coaxial circles, a ruled B-spline otherwise (a primitive only when the contacts lie on it within tol). Refused (`NonGeneric`): a radius beyond a radius of curvature of a face, a contact off its face (out at the middle of the edge, or leaving it across an edge other than those at the vertices: past an acute corner, the faces at the ends cut it), faces tangent along the edge (a fillet vanishing where they become tangent at a vertex is given, its contacts meeting there, and ends there, not continued past it), an edge convex in places and concave in others, any other failure (a curve evaluated beyond its bounds); `InvalidId`: an edge not of the solid, not shared by two faces, a seam, the face of d1 not of the edge; `InvalidBounds`: a radius or a distance not positive, tolerances not positive, a negative extension. Python: `brep.fillet_geometry`, `brep.chamfer_geometry`, `brep.FilletOptions`, `brep.EdgeBlend`, `brep.BlendContact`. * `gbs-brep/blend.h`: vertex blends (stage 6, PR 3, `docs/sources/design/brep6_pr03_vertex_blends.md`). At a vertex whose n edges are all filleted and whose sphere is not exact (radii that differ, more than three edges, a radius dropping toward the corner, convex and concave edges) or where setbacks are asked, one B-spline face tangent to the fillets and the faces round it: each fillet ends on its section where the farther contact of its neighbours crosses its own (or farther: `FilletOptions::setbacks`, `Setback(vertex, edge, distance)` along the edge, a suitcase corner), Hermite joining curves on the faces between contacts that end apart, the hole filled by `fill_n_sided`. Where two contacts would end nearly at one point, both end at their crossing (radii nearly equal: no tiny side, the blend tends to the sphere as the radii agree, 4e-5 off at 1e-4) or both fillets are set back by a quarter of their radius (contacts oblique between different radii: sections that cannot meet G1 on a C2 surface); the end of a fillet between two joining curves shorter than a tenth of the hole is set back until they are that long (two short sides round one end fold the fill: radii 0.5, 0.51 and 0.52 were refused after 30 s); a setback asked is kept, never brought to a crossing, and refused at once where it would leave a joining curve under 7e-3 of the hole, or two under 5e-2 round its fillet. G1 within 1e-5 along every side; the same faces as OCCT on a box corner of three radii, 0.014 more taken off. The corner of an L and a radius dropping toward a corner, refused until now, are vertex blends. Five gallery cases. Python: `brep.Setback`, `FilletOptions.setbacks` (a tuple, set by assigning a list).

* `gbs/nsided.h` (new): `fill_n_sided(ribbons, NSidedOptions)`, the surface filling an n-sided hole tangent to the surfaces round it (stage 6, PR 2, `docs/sources/design/brep6_pr02_nsided.md`). * `gbs-brep/blend.h`: blends meeting at a vertex (stage 6, PR 1, `docs/sources/design/brep_palier6_vertex_blends.md`, `docs/sources/design/brep6_pr01_mitres.md`). The curve across a blend where it meets another surface shared by the free ends and the mitres (`cross_curve`); ends of several edges (`End::pieces`). Volumes against references worked out on their own (the overlap of two fillets integrated, inclusion-exclusion of chamfers, Steiner's formula for the rounded pyramid: 1e-9 to 1e-12, 1e-8); beside OCCT, the same solids. Five gallery cases.
* `gbs-brep/blend.h`, `gbs-brep/fillet.h`: fillets of a varying radius on the solid (stage 5, PR 2, `docs/sources/design/brep5_pr02_variable_topology.md`). `fillet(model, solid, edges, RadiusLaw, FilletOptions)`: the law runs along each chain of tangent edges given (by the arc length along the chain); the fillet in pieces where the centre line of the balls has a corner (a kink of a linear law, or the curvature of the edge jumping while the radius varies: a straight edge meeting a circle, the vertex of a closed edge that is not C2). There the balls next to the point cover the middle of the lune of its ball between its two characteristic circles, and the envelopes meet along a crease (a peak of the law: `EdgeBlend::creases`, marched across the pieces), or they do not, and the lune is part of the fillet (a hollow); the lunes are faces of spheres oriented against the faces of their pieces. At a vertex of a chain, past which the law of each edge goes on with its own slope, the joint of the two fillets decides (fillets with a law only: chamfers and fillets of constant radius that do not join there stay refused), both continued past it by 1.5 radius at least whatever `extension`. The supports, their reach and the folding of the offsets are checked for the largest radius of each edge, its own, and for the pieces continued past a peak with their slope. Corners where the radius grows toward the corner (the ends of the fillets small circles of the ball of the corner); a radius dropping toward a corner refused. Round a closed B-spline face that is not periodic (the root of a blade), the loop starts on the seam of the face. Behaviour changes
* loft: the skin-direction (v) parameters now default to the averaged chord-length of NURBS Book §10.3 instead of a uniform parametrization (#71) — lofted surfaces differ from 0.4.x for the same input
* `find_span` now follows NURBS Book A2.1 exactly (half-open convention `k[s] <= u < k[s+1]`, #42/#46): correct one-sided derivatives at knots of multiplicity > 1

Performance
* evaluation: allocation-free O(p²) evaluator and one-pass multi-order derivatives (#30); span reduction enabled for derivatives (~12x faster curve derivatives); per-call eval ~4x faster than OCCT
* bulk evaluators are parallel above a size threshold (#88, 12–30x over OCCT's scalar loop); grid sampling (`offset_points`, surface `discretize`) pre-sized and parallel (#89)
* Python: bulk evaluators return numpy arrays without copy (#97, ~10x at 1M points)
* interpolation: banded / sparse / separable solves (#34), then direct band assembly + no-pivot band LU (#96): ~10x on an 800-point build, now faster than OCCT and scipy at every tested size
* approximation: banded one-pass assembly and structured least-squares solve (#65, 2.5–9x vs OCCT)
* loft: v-system factorized once, batched pole solves (#40)
* new `build_batch` (`gbs/execution.h`): builds N independent interpolations/approximations in parallel, exceptions propagated to the caller (#91)
* mesh: `elliptic_structured_smoothing` sweeps rows in parallel, bit-identical to the serial loop (#90, 5x at 10k and 16x at 1M interior vertices on 64 cores)

New features
* degree reduction (NURBS Book A5.11, #78): `BSCurve::reduceDegree(tol)`, `BSSurface::reduceDegreeU/V(tol)` return (success, rigorous error bound) and leave the geometry untouched when not reducible within `tol`; Python bindings, plus `BSSurface.increaseDegreeU/V`
* hodograph (NURBS Book A3.3, #83): `derivative_curve(crv, k)` returns the k-th derivative of a non-rational curve as a `BSCurve` (C++ and Python)
* rational surface derivatives (NURBS Book A4.4, #33)
* spine-guided rational loft, and `loft_approx` (well-posed loft by approximation, #58/#63)
* vectorized derivatives w.r.t. curvilinear abscissa: curves `d_dms` / `d_dm2s`, surfaces `d_dmus` / `d_dmvs` / `d_dmu2s` / `d_dmv2s`
* Python bindings: `d_dm` / `d_dm2` (curves) and `d_dmu` / `d_dmv` / `d_dmu2` / `d_dmv2` (surfaces) accept a list of parameters
* `pygbs.gbs.__version__`; the installed CMake package now carries its version (`find_package(GBS 0.5)`)

Fixes
* `remove_knot` faithful to NURBS Book A5.8 for `num > 1` (#59)
* `refine_approx` stop criteria and pole-count guards (#65)
* rational `loft` with spine compiles and interpolates; rational `flat_v` overload returns a surface (#40/#58)
* hardened Bowyer-Watson cavity construction in the 2D Delaunay mesher (#48)
* gbs-occt: OpenCASCADE 8 support (7.x still supported), 2 runtime divergences fixed (#47)
* Python: `elliptic_structured_smoothing` ignored `n_it` and `tol` (a single sweep was done); `BSSurface.reverseV` reversed U; `vistaplot` ready for pyvista 0.50 (`copy(deep=...)`)

Build / tooling
* tests migrated from GoogleTest to doctest; test executables are no longer installed
* tuning constants centralized in `gbs/gbsconstants.h` (#38)
* optional precompiled headers (`GBS_USE_PCH`), coverage build (`GBS_COVERAGE`)
* CI on Linux, Windows, macOS arm64 (+ Intel, non-blocking) and OCCT 7/8
* the in-repo conda recipe is removed: gbs is packaged by conda-forge (gbs-feedstock)
* audit reports and benchmarks under `bench/` (NURBS Book fidelity, interpolation, approximation, parallelization, OCCT/scipy/geomdl comparison)
