# GBS Release Notes
Unreleased

Features
* `gbs/bscanalysis.h`: `area_properties` gives the area enclosed by a closed chain of planar curves (a section contour, its pieces in order) and the centroid of that surface, by Green's theorem about the chain's first point. Each piece runs either way, from where the previous one ends (the sides of a section may both run from its leading edge); the order of the pieces gives the direction of the chain. A gap between pieces is closed by a straight segment, up to `max_gap` (by default any), a piece of zero length adds nothing. Each piece is integrated on the spans where it is smooth (`smooth_spans`), one evaluation of C and C' per node: 15 points of Gauss-Kronrod, exact on a polynomial B-spline up to degree 7, halved where they do not converge on another curve. The area is signed (positive counterclockwise), the centroid does not depend on the direction; a chain that encloses no area (twice its area within the rounding of its coordinates, 16 eps ∮ (|o| + |C|) |C'|, whatever the unit), a null curve or a gap above `max_gap` throws `std::invalid_argument`. The pieces as for `curvature_comb`. `AreaProperties`: the area and the centroid
* `gbs/bscanalysis.h`: `curvature_comb` gives the curvature comb of a chain of planar curves (a section contour, its pieces in order): teeth on the normal of length proportional to the square root of the signed curvature, one scale for every piece (the longest tooth `length` long), `teeth` shared out by length then refined by `deviation_based_params` where the curve turns; outwards where a chain run counterclockwise is convex, so a tooth inwards is a curvature inversion. The pieces are any range (a view too) of curves, of pointers or of shared pointers, or a braced list of pointers (T deduced), or a single curve; a null one throws `std::invalid_argument`; one evaluation of C, C', C'' per tooth, a fixed Gauss rule for the share, no tangent below 1e-9 of a piece's mean speed (any unit). `CurvatureCombPiece`: the teeth as one polyline and their envelope
* `gbs/curvebreaks.h`: `smooth_spans(curve, a, b)` gives the ends of the spans of [a, b] on which a curve is smooth: cut at the knots of a B-spline (within its bounds, also when trimmed without dropping its knots; none within `knot_eps` of a bound, so that a bound an ulp off a knot makes no span of an ulp), at the joins of a `CurveComposite` and at the breaks of its curves, at those of the curve under a `CurveTrimmed` or a `CurveReversed`. `deviation_based_params` takes them as hard points (the joins of a composite, where it took only the knots of a B-spline), and `CurvePieces` samples a curve that is not a B-spline span by span (the intersections, projections and meshes of composite curves start from pieces cut at their joins)
* `gbs/bscanalysis.h`: `signed_curvature` of a planar curve ((C' x C'') / |C'|^3, positive turning left), from the curve at u or from C' and C''; `curvature` of a planar curve is its absolute value. `left_normal` of a planar direction, used by `normal_direction`

Fixes
* `gbs/intersect/curves.h` (review of the curve-curve intersection, `docs/sources/design/brep2_pr01_intersect_curves.md` § 7): the seeds of a common portion no longer merge across the seam of a closed curve (the same circle with its seams at 0 and 1 rad gave 9 tangent crossings, now two overlaps meeting at the seam); a portion is grown to where the curves part and is an overlap when the gap between the curves is not a quadratic of the parameter or keeps a core besides its tangent departures (a line and the same line tilted by 5e-7, a straight part followed by a tangent arc: overlaps, where they were tangent crossings); Newton goes to a local minimum of the distance (step halved, the 2 x 2 system scaled by its own diagonal, out of the saddle between two crossings) and crossings between which the curves stay within the tolerance are one tangent contact (a line 1e-6 under the top of a unit circle gave 24 crossings); a crossing at the seam of a closed curve is reported once (157 of 800 configurations gave it twice); a seed needs parallel tangents (a quintic crossing the axis at the 5 samples was a common portion); flat, nearly parallel pieces are followed along their distance instead of being halved down to 10 tol (concentric circles 1.5 tol apart now complete in under 0.02 s up to R = 1000, the cap was reached from R = 10) and the budget of pairs is shared among the initial pairs; `gbs-brep/coincident.h`, `section.h` and `partial.h` report a capped intersection (`complete` false) instead of using it (two boolean tests relied on one). `CurvePieces` puts a bound a few ulps off a knot on it (its Bézier segments were lost); `smooth_spans` and `deviation_based_params` (#189) compare with knot_eps relative to the parameters (a span of an ulp came back from 128 on) and keep a break on a uniform parameter once (`discretize` gave a point twice)
* `gbs/quadrature.h`: `integrate_kronrod`, Boost's adaptive Gauss-Kronrod rule taken on the variable of [-1, 1]. Boost compares the error of its rule on [-1, 1], not scaled, with a tolerance scaled by the half-width of the interval: an interval narrower than about 3e-8 was bisected down to the depth limit whatever the integrand (983 025 evaluations of a constant), a wide one stopped before reaching its tolerance. `length` uses it (and so `abs_curv`, its adaptive refinement, which halves intervals, and the TFI meshes of `gbs-mesh`): over a range of 1e-8, 31 evaluations instead of 2 031 585 (57 ms); the length of a curve no longer depends on its bounds (over [0, 1e3] it was 2e-8 off)
* `gbs/intersect/curve_surface.h` (review of the curve-surface intersection, `docs/sources/design/brep2_pr02_curve_surface.md` § 7): a curve lying on a curved or tilted surface no longer exhausts `max_pairs` (pairs also culled by the slab of the plane of each patch, a piece seeded when its points project on the whole box, pairs inside a seed skipped: a generatrix of a tilted cylinder gives its portion [-5, 5] instead of a truncated one or 2 816 hits); a tangency or a near miss through subdivision is one tangent crossing (not 14 portions or 69 hits), a curve within the tolerance over its whole length a portion; the closed forms of a line no longer depend on its parameter origin (reparametrized from the foot of the frame origin; `polynomial_roots` drops a leading coefficient by its term on the interval: rays from 1e4 away against a torus, knots at 1e7), take the tolerance into account (a line 1e-7 from a plane, a generatrix of a cylinder of radius 100: portions), test the ends of the range (crossings there were lost 40 % of the time) and keep the candidates at a pole or on the other nappe of a cone; Gauss-Newton stopped on the pole of a NURBS sphere starts again around it; a closed curve crossing at its start is reported once. `gbs-brep/classify.h` never counts a ray whose intersection was capped (`complete` false): another direction, `BRepError` (or `nullopt` for a face against a solid) when every ray is
* `gbs-brep/model.h` (review of the BREP data model, `docs/sources/design/brep_pr01_model.md` § 8): `Model::append` of a model to itself copies it once (the copy loops grew the tables they read: a crash, also from Python); `Model::name` returns a copy (the view it returned read freed memory after an `erase` or a `compact`); the accessors are const or not as the model, by deducing this (C++23), without the `const_cast`
* `gbs/intersect/analytic_surfaces.h`, `surface_surface.h`, `gbs/primitives.h` (review of the closed-form surface intersections, `docs/sources/design/brep2_pr03_analytic.md` § 7): two planes at less than 1e-8 rad no longer give a line at infinity (an exception ending a boolean) nor, far from the origin, a line off the planes or none; a spindle torus keeps the part of its tube across the axis, and `ConicalSurface::parameters` / `ToroidalSurface::parameters` give u + pi where the radial factor is negative (beyond the apex of a cone, inside a spindle torus); parallel and coaxial pairs are decided by the gap they make across the common region of the boxes, not below 1e-10 rad (the same cylinder with its axis tilted by 1e-8 is coincident); ellipses are clipped to that region like lines, and a plane frame far from the quadric no longer cancels the conic; pcurves are sampled more densely where they turn fast (a circle passing near a pole) and judged on their fit, so that tangencies and apexes accepted within tol and planes through the apex of a cone are complete; crossings with the bounds are polished on the curve itself
* `gbs-brep/explore.h` (review, `brep_pr02_explore.md` § 8): `explore` costs the size of the shape explored, not of the model: the visited ids in a hash set, a dense table once a 32nd of the model is visited (an edge in a model of 40 000: 0.09 µs instead of 1.33). `TopologyIndex::coedges_of` also gives the co-edges of the free wires
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h` (review, `brep2_pr04_marching.md` § 7): a loop is closed only by the step that passes its start (on the step, tangents and parameters alike): a thin loop is no longer closed across its width, nor marched around two to four times from a start near an extremum of a parameter; a start is on a traced curve only within the sag of its steps and with its parameters (a second line 2e-3 from a straight one is traced, and so is the short piece of a plane/cylinder section beyond the seam); small loops are found whatever the parametrization (start search in lengths, a pair halved again when its start lands away); narrow detours are followed and a landing step no longer crosses the apex of a cone to the other nappe (sag of each step, the last one too, checked at its middle and quarters); close parallel faces are pruned by the slabs of their patches instead of exhausting `max_pairs`; the correctors converge far from the origin (stop relative to the rounding), and circles of a few dozen tol are traced (a larger turn kept at the floor of the step when its sag is within tol / 2)
* `gbs-io/iges_brep.h` (review, `step_pr02_attributes.md` § 7): a name longer than the 8 columns of an IGES label (libIGES truncates it: `pressure_side_0001` and `pressure_side_0002` both became `pressure`) is also written whole, as a Name property (entity 406, form 15)
* `gbs-brep/builders.h`, `pcurve.h`, `closure.h` (review of the builders of edges, wires and faces, `brep_pr05_face_wire.md` § 8, `brep_pr03_builders_wire.md` § 8): a seam co-edge between two poles takes the side given by the sense of the face (`MakeFaceOptions::sense`, passed by the STEP reader from `same_sense`) instead of always u1, so the two halves of a sphere cut through its seam are two different halves; a surface closed within `pcurve_tol` has a seam (a NURBS cylinder open by 3e-6 gave a face whose (u, v) loop jumped a period); a hole near a curved outer boundary is tested on a polygon refined where needed; an exact pcurve on a seam is moved by a period to the side of its neighbours; `make_wire` keeps both ends of an edge shorter than the merge tolerance; `make_edge` on given vertices rejects a curve that is not finite at its bounds
* `gbs-brep/mesh.h`, `pcurve.h` (review, `brep_palier3_mesh.md` § 10, `brep_pr05_face_wire.md` § 9): the mesh of a cone is stable around any deflection. A triangle with a corner at a pole or an apex was measured in (u, v), where that corner may lie on another generatrix than its 3D sides: slivers along the seam of a cone showed a deviation their 3D triangles do not have, and the centroid they were given folded a triangle at the apex, which no point inserted in it can unfold (`cone.stp` at its default deflection times 1 + 2e-7: 196 414 triangles in 16 s, incomplete and folded, instead of 1 948 in 3 ms). Such a triangle is now measured where it is in 3D, the corner taking the free parameter of the other two (`FaceMesh::free`), and its centroid stays between their generatrices; the normal at a pole is taken there too. Without `min_angle`, a cone is now meshed by the exact fan from its apex (126 triangles instead of 1 810). A projected pcurve is also measured at the quarters of its spans and at the top of the parabola through the three gaps: the tolerance of its edge was up to 9 % under its deviation (2.402e-6 for 2.625e-6 on a circle of radius 1), it is now within 0.03 %
* `gbs-brep/classify.h`, `section.h`, `split.h`, `edit.h` (review of the classification, the section and the split, `brep2_pr05_classify.md` § 7, `brep2_pr06_section.md` § 8, `brep2_pr07_split.md` § 7): the (u, v) box of a face encloses its pcurves (the poles of their Bézier pieces) instead of being the box of 17 samples of each, which cut a curved boundary: points of a disk near its rim were outside it, and so were points of its prism near the rim and the top, and a plane cutting the disk there gave no section; a point is classified against a solid with these boxes computed once instead of for every ray (2.0 ms instead of 2.3 against 880 faces). `split_face` splits cuts crossing each other at a common vertex (a square cut by a cross stayed one face with slits, a box split by two crossing planes had 10 faces instead of 16). A split face is replaced by its pieces in its compounds too (it was lost from them), and `split_edge` puts the second half of an edge after it in the compounds holding it; the pieces keep the external id of the face. A section piece running from a vertex to another of a face boundary within the confusion radius of the edges between them (10 tol) is these edges (a plane 1.5 tol below the top of a box gave no section, 2.5 tol below new edges sharing the vertices of the top ones)
* `gbs-brep/boolean.h`, `coincident.h`, `partial.h`, `classify.h`, `split.h` (review of the booleans, `brep2_pr09_boolean.md` § 7, `brep2_pr09b_coincident.md` § 7, `brep2_pr07_split.md` § 7): section edges meeting at a vertex are no longer split there as a crossing (a regression of the previous entry: the cylinder and the cone of the tests overlapping by 0.3 failed in fuse, common and cut); a cavity of the result goes to the smallest solid holding a point of it, not the smallest whose box holds it (a cube with a cavity inside a thin ring gave the cavity to the ring); a failed boolean erases everything it added, also when a step throws `BRepError` (now `NonGeneric`, and an operand that cannot be copied no longer throws); the solids of the result are made from their shells oriented once (the volume of each shell was computed twice: a fuse of 1472 faces in 4.3 s instead of 6.5); `FaceClassifier` classifies many points of a face with its boxes computed once, edges far from the point not projected on (two stacked plates with 36 crossing holes each fused in 6.3 s instead of 31); the imprint of coincident faces fails when it reaches its bound of steps instead of stopping unfinished
* `gbs-brep/check.h`, `sew.h`, `closure.h`, `explore.h`, `pcurve.h` (review of the check, the sewing and the solids, `brep_pr06_check.md` § 7, `brep_pr07_sew.md` § 7, `brep_pr08_solid.md` § 7): `check` tests the inclusion of holes as `make_face` does (`UVRegion`: refined near a curved boundary; a disk with a hole reaching 0.99 of its radius was reported `InnerWireOutsideOuter`), and reports `VoidOutsideOuter`, a new issue, when a cavity of a solid is not inside its outer shell; `sew` measures the deviation of a recomposed pcurve off its samples too (`detail::interpolation_gap`, shared with `project_pcurve`: the edge tolerance was up to 31 % under it); `face_volume_integral` finds its cells row by row, the same cells summed in the same order (O(n²) per face instead of O(n³): `check` of a solid of 880 faces in 0.06 s instead of 1.3, the volume of the booleans, of `make_solid` and of the STEP reader as much faster); `shell_edge_uses` and the sewing follow the order of the edge ids, not that of a hash table (the order of the reports of `check` and of the faces in the sewn shells no longer depends on the standard library)

v0.6.0 — 21 oct 2026

Breaking
* `gbs/bscanalysis.h`: `compute_rolling_ball` computes the largest circle tangent to crv1 at u1 that holds no point of crv2: its radius is the minimum over crv2 of rho(q) = |q - p|² / (2 (q - p).n), the circle tangent at p through q (samples on every knot span, Brent, the ball certified by the global projection of its centre on crv2, the contact solved by TOMS 748). Near the end of a curve the ball now rests on that end (a contact on a corner, u2 at the bound) where it threw `std::runtime_error` (no root of the difference of the radii) or, with the normals parallel within 1e-3 at the projection, returned the midpoint of p and of the projection, a circle tangent to neither curve; on the leading edge of a tbs blade passage it also returned centres at 1e15 (a pole of the radii taken for a root). **Breaking:** it throws `std::runtime_error` where the only circles tangent to both curves hold points of crv1 (on the back of a circle facing another one, where it returned them), and the third value only reports whether the normals are parallel at the projection: the ball is computed the same way. As fast on the tbs case (9.9 µs instead of 9.4). `max_curvature_pos` and the rolling ball share their samples (`analysis_detail::span_samples`), and their Brent searches are bounded to 100 iterations
* NLopt is no longer a dependency of gbs (third and last step of `docs/sources/design/nlopt_removal.md`): `gbs/solvers.h` (`solve_N_nlop`, `solve_D_nlop`) and `default_nlopt_algo` are removed, and so are the `solver` parameters of the extrema (`gbs/extrema.h`) and of `max_curvature_pos`, the `n_bracket` parameters of the curve extrema (`extrema_curve_point`, `extrema_curve_curve`, `extrema_curve_curve_lst`; those of the surface extrema remain, for surfaces outside 3D), and in Python the `solver` argument and the `nlopt_algorithm` enum. CMake no longer looks for NLopt (nor does `GbsConfig.cmake`), and the CI no longer installs it; the Boost and Eigen headers remain the only compile-time dependencies of the core

Features
* `docs/sources/examples`: visual examples of the native BREP (first step of stage 4, `docs/sources/design/brep_palier4.md`): `gbs_examples.py` builds primitive solids with the BREP API (box, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, exact or NURBS) and the boolean cases of the OpenCascade comparison, meshes them and renders them with PyVista (one color per face, the edges of the BREP); `booleans.ipynb` and `meshing.ipynb` show them interactively; `gallery.py` renders them off screen with `gallery.md`, and the CI publishes this gallery as the `gallery` artifact of each build. `python/tests/test_examples.py` keeps every case valid. Design of stage 4 (sweep along a path, offsets and thick solids, fillets and chamfers)
* `gbs-brep/mesh.h`: meshes of shapes, last step of the BREP meshing (stage 3 completed, `docs/sources/design/brep_palier3_mesh.md`, review in § 9): `mesh(model, shape, options)` gives a `Mesh` (vertices, triangles facing outwards, the triangles of each face, (u, v) and the normal of the surface at each corner), watertight along the shared edges; `area`, `volume`, `open_sides`. The parameter space of a face is scaled by the spacing its curvature asks for in each direction (triangles long along the generatrices of a cylinder), consecutive pieces of an edge are merged while within the options (an approximated intersection curve no longer gets a point per knot), and an edge is refined until the chords of its pcurves follow its faces. `MeshOptions::min_angle`; `Mesh::folded` reports faces with a triangle against the normal of their surface. `gbs-io/mesh_writer.h`: STL (binary and ASCII) and OBJ, numbers in their shortest exact form with a point whatever the locale (`gbs-io/real_format.h`, shared with the STEP writer). `gbs-render/vtkMeshRender.h`: VTK polydata and actor with the normals of the surfaces. Python: `gbs.brep.mesh`, `MeshOptions`, `Mesh` (numpy arrays), `write_stl`, `write_obj`, `gbs.make_actor(mesh)`, `vistaplot.mesh_to_polydata`. Checked against `BRepMesh_IncrementalMesh` of OpenCascade: closed meshes, within the deflection where OCCT exceeds it on a sphere and a torus, triangles of the same order
* `gbs-brep/mesh.h`: triangulation of the faces (stage 3 of the BREP meshing): `MeshBuilder::face` gives a `FaceMesh`. The boundary of the face is constrained in a Delaunay triangulation of its parameter space, scaled by the mean lengths of S_u and S_v, then refined inside, never on the boundary, until each triangle is within the deflection, the angle and `max_edge`, and is not folded against the surface. A triangle gets its circumcentre, or its centroid when the circumcentre is outside the face or near its boundary; triangles under 20° get their circumcentre when it is well inside. Seams and poles close the mesh: the triangles along a pole, which have no area, are left out. `MeshOptions::max_points` bounds the points of a face; a face that does not triangulate gives `NonGeneric`. `ConstrainedDelaunay` tells the triangles and the points of its domain before `remove_outside` (`domain`, `place`, `has_triangle`)
* `gbs-mesh/cdt.h`: `ConstrainedDelaunay`, a constrained Delaunay triangulation of the plane written in the repository (stage 3 of the BREP meshing): incremental Bowyer-Watson whose cavities do not cross the constrained edges (a point on one splits it), recovery of the constrained edges by flips (Sloan) then back to Delaunay, removal of the triangles outside the domain by the parity of the constraints crossed (holes included); Shewchuk's exact predicates, `incircle` added to `inc/topology/robustPredicates.h`
* `gbs-brep/mesh.h`: first step of the BREP meshing (stage 3, `docs/sources/design/brep_palier3_mesh.md`): `MeshOptions` (deflection, angle, longest segment), `curve_parameters` (the knots of the curve, intervals halved until within the options), `MeshBuilder` sharing the discretization of each edge between the faces that use it, and the boundaries of the faces in their parameter space (seams twice with the same vertices, the points of a pole one vertex). `BuildErrc::MissingPCurve`
* `gbs/minimize.h`: `nelder_mead`, minimization without derivatives of a function of a few variables in a box (Nelder-Mead simplex), for the objectives `solve_N_nlop` served outside gbs (shape parameters fitted to angles, non-smooth sums of absolute values, distances in a transformed space): bounds held by a change of variables (x = lo + (hi - lo)(1 + sin z)/2, lo + z², or free; trial points kept in the period of the best vertex), restarts from the best point while f decreases, NaN taken as +inf, a budget of evaluations, the initial step relative to the box (a tenth by default: it chooses among the basins around x0), T deduced from the arrays only, f called non-const (a `mutable` lambda, a stateful functor), no dependency. Design: `docs/sources/design/nelder_mead.md`
* `gbs-brep/partial.h`, `gbs-brep/boolean.h`: booleans of faces coincident on a part of their overlap (a B-spline face lying on another surface over some of its patches, parting from it or crossing it beyond) instead of `NonGeneric`: a Bézier patch being analytic, the common region is a union of patches of the B-spline face, bounded by its knot lines; the face is split along them (exact iso lines) before the section, its piece on the other surface is then coincident with it, and its pieces off it are intersected with it only on the patches where the gap changes sign (`SectionOptions::restricted`). Checked against OpenCascade, which does not see the contact
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h`: a tangency along a curve that ends inside the faces is accepted where the surfaces part beyond its end (`Marcher::End::parted`, an edge with a free end that splits no face). The tangency ends where the surfaces stop bending alike, whatever the tolerance; beyond, the extremum of the gap across the curve keeps the sign of the relative curvature when they part, and a tangency that keeps within the tolerance up to a border lands there; where it changes sign the surfaces cross, two transversal curves start there and the intersection stays singular (`NonGeneric` booleans)
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h`: surfaces tangent along a curve without a closed form (a cylinder lying on a B-spline face, a torus in a cylinder, a sphere in a cylinder of its radius) are traced instead of `singular`: `TangentMarcher` follows the solutions of the foot of S1 on S2 and of parallel normals, a system of rank 3 on the curve, in the direction where the second fundamental forms agree; the curves are flagged `tangent`, so booleans of such solids work instead of `NonGeneric` (checked against OpenCascade with the faces of the box converted to NURBS). A landing where a curve only touches a bound is put on the contact exactly (`Marcher::touch`)
* `gbs/intersect/projection.h`: closest points by Newton's method in any dimension (point-curve, point-surface, curve-curve, curve-surface) on bounded ranges: full Hessian (quadratic convergence also at a non-zero distance), Gauss-Newton where it is not positive definite, variables held on an active bound, step halved until the distance does not increase; global versions by subdivision (`global_point_curve`, `global_point_surface`, `global_curve_curve`, `global_curve_surface`): the pieces of the geometries (Bézier pieces of B-splines, enclosed by the boxes of their poles) are examined by increasing lower bound (boxes, slabs around their chords and planes), dropped when they cannot be closer than the best point found, halved until flat, and Newton starts from each flat piece, so that the global minimum is found where a grid of 30 samples missed it; sampled versions from the two nearest samples of a grid (surfaces outside 3D, projections inside the intersections). The extrema of `gbs/extrema.h` use the global versions; their `n_bracket` parameters are ignored for curves. First step of the removal of NLopt (`docs/sources/design/nlopt_removal.md`)
* `gbs-brep/pcurve.h`: exact pcurves of B-spline edges on planes (`plane_pcurve`): the poles mapped into the parameters of the plane, homogeneous coordinates kept for a rational curve, same knots; an edge following an iso line of a B-spline surface (a curve on it, as the edges of natural faces) is recognized as that B-spline (`detail::as_bspline`). Used when a face is built (`project_pcurve`), when faces are sewn, and by the imprint of coincident faces: the caps of a rational B-spline cylinder get pcurves of 9 poles instead of interpolations of 513
* `gbs/intersect/surface_surface.h`: the closed-form curves (lines, circles, ellipses) are restricted to the parameter boxes exactly: the carrier is cut at the apexes and poles it goes through, and on each span the roots of each coordinate of its pcurves minus the bounds of the box, repeated every turn for a periodic parameter, are found by Bézier subdivision in one dimension (touches included), instead of a sampling of 512 steps that could miss a part shorter than a step (a circle grazing a narrow face). Stage 2 bis completed (`docs/sources/design/brep_palier2bis.md`, review in § 12): 22 pairs of solids × 3 operations checked against OpenCascade
* `gbs-brep/section.h`, `gbs-brep/coincident.h`, `gbs-brep/boolean.h`: coincident faces of any surfaces (B-splines, a primitive and a B-spline of the same shape): detected by projecting a grid of points of each face on the surface of the other (faces touching along their borders when their intersection is singular there), imprinted with pcurves projected on the surfaces and seams read from the closure of the surface; edges lying on B-spline faces are imprinted the same way. Booleans of coplanar and stacked B-spline boxes, and of a box with planes against a B-spline box, checked against OpenCascade (operands converted to NURBS by OCCT)
* `gbs-brep/split.h`: a cut ending at a pole or an apex splits the degenerate edge there (`split_degenerate`), so that faces of spheres and cones are split by curves through their poles and apexes: booleans of a sphere centred on the side of a cylinder (both poles on the section), of Viviani's sphere and cylinder with the poles on the curve, of a cone cut through its apex (also when its seam lies on the cutting plane), checked against OpenCascade
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h`, `gbs-brep/section.h`: intersection curves crossing at branch points (equal crossed cylinders, the double point of Viviani's curve) are no longer `singular`: the marching steps straight over a branch point, onto the same branch beyond a dip of the angle between the normals (also when an ordinary step goes through it, recognized by n1 x n2 turning back, or when a seam passes through it), and reports the spans it stepped over (`IntersectionCurve::branch_spans`, Python `branch_spans`); the section crosses these spans and cuts the curves there, so that booleans of such solids work, with the numbers of solids and faces of OpenCascade. Tangencies along a curve still stop the marching
* `gbs-brep/boolean.h`, `gbs-brep/coincident.h`: booleans where an edge of one operand lies on a face of the other (a box resting on an edge, boxes touching along a part of an edge, the seam of a cylinder lying on a plane) instead of `NonGeneric`: `imprint_edge` splits the edge and the boundary of the face where they cross, merges the pieces running along the boundary with its edges and gives the pieces inside the face as cuts; same numbers of solids and faces as OpenCascade. `SectionResult::edges_on_faces` reports each edge lying on a face of the other shape with that face
* `gbs-brep/boolean.h`: booleans of solids touching at a point or along a line (sphere on a box, cylinder lying on a box, slivers touching along a line of tangency) instead of `NonGeneric`: contact points split nothing, edges of tangency are section edges that may stay inside a face they only touch, and where more than two kept faces meet at an edge they are paired around it (each with its neighbour across its material, tangent faces ordered by their curvature), every pair but the first getting its own copy of the edge so that shells stay manifold; same numbers of solids and faces as OpenCascade. First step of stage 2 bis (`docs/sources/design/brep_palier2bis.md`)
* `gbs-brep/classify.h`: `interior_points`, several interior points of a face; the position of a face against a solid uses the first one off the boundary of the solid
* Python: sub-module `gbs.intersect` (`curves`, `curve_surface`, `surfaces`) and stage 2 of `gbs.brep` (`classify`, `interior_point`, `section`, `split_face`, `split`, `boolean`, `fuse`, `cut`, `common`, `make_prism`, `make_revolution`, `offset_face`)
* Stage 2 of the native BREP checked by OpenCascade (`gbs-occt/tests/tests_brep2_occt.cpp`): 21 booleans (boxes, box and cylinder, box and sphere, crossed cylinders, coplanar, stacked and coaxial operands) give valid shapes with the volumes and numbers of faces of `BRepAlgoAPI`; sections have the lengths of `BRepAlgoAPI_Section`; prisms and revolutions the volumes of `BRepPrimAPI`
* `gbs-brep/sweep.h`: `make_prism` and `make_revolution` of an edge (face), a wire (shell) or a face (solid): each edge gives a natural face of its swept surface, a primitive when possible (plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus), the exact NURBS extrusion or revolution otherwise; edges on the axis sweep nothing; the caps are copies of the profile and the faces are sewn
* `gbs-brep/offset.h`: `offset_face`, the face at a distance along the normal of its surface, keeping its parametrization so that its pcurves are reused: exact offset primitives (a cone shifts its pcurves), `OffsetSurface` otherwise; the apex of a cone becomes a closed edge
* `gbs-brep/coincident.h`, `gbs-brep/boolean.h`: booleans with coincident faces (same plane, coaxial primitives of the same radius): the faces are imprinted on each other (edges split at their crossings, coincident vertices and overlapping edges merged, edges of one inside the other as cuts), and their coincident pieces are kept once or dropped according to the sides of the solids (stacked, shifted, identical boxes; coaxial cylinders)
* `gbs-brep/boolean.h`: boolean operations on solids (`fuse`, `cut`, `common`, `boolean(op, a, b)`), on copies of the operands: section, split of the faces of both copies by the same edges, classification of every face against the other operand, selection, assembly of shells by shared edges and of solids with their cavities; generic configurations only, the others (coincident or tangent faces, edges or vertices on the other operand, singular intersections) give the new `BuildErrc::NonGeneric`
* `gbs-brep/split.h`: `split_face` splits a face by edges crossing it, in its parameter space (boundary edges split where cuts end on them, in every wire using them; arrangement of half-edges with the two sides of a seam as distinct nodes; loops of positive area as outer boundaries, holes given to the smallest loop containing them; dangling cuts reported); the pieces share the surface and replace the face in its shells. `split(shape, tool)` splits the faces of a shape along its section by a shape or a surface: solids stay closed
* `gbs-brep/section.h`: section of two shapes, or of a shape by a surface: the edges where their faces meet, with their pcurves on both faces, restricted to the inside of both faces, sharing the vertices of the shapes within the tolerance, chained into closed or open wires in a compound; edges of the shapes lying in the section (along a face boundary) and coincident faces reported
* `gbs-brep/classify.h`: position of a point against a face, in its parameter space (ray crossed with the pcurves of the wires), and against a solid with its cavities (ray crossed with the faces); `On` within the tolerance of the boundary; ambiguous rays (through a vertex or an edge, tangent, along a face) retried in a fixed sequence of directions; `interior_point` of a face and position of a face against a solid
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h`: intersection of any two surfaces by marching: starting points from the subdivision of both surfaces into flat patches, prediction along n1 x n2 and Newton correction in the plane normal to the step, step adapted to the turn of the tangent, landing on the boundaries of the boxes, closed loops, singular points (tangency, branch point) flagged; curves as cubic B-splines with their two pcurves on the same parameter, densified until within the tolerance. `intersect(s1, box1, s2, box2)` now handles every pair (`IntersectOptions::closed_forms` to force marching); results in `gbs/intersect/intersection_curve.h` with `SurfaceSurfaceResult::singular`
* `gbs/intersect/surface_surface.h`: intersection of two surfaces on parameter boxes, as curves carrying a 3D curve and a pcurve on each surface on the same parameter, contact points and coincidence. Pairs of primitives with a closed form (`gbs/intersect/analytic_surfaces.h`): two planes, plane and cylinder or cone (circle, ellipse, generatrices), plane and sphere, two spheres, coaxial surfaces of revolution (circles from their meridians), plane through the axis of a torus, cylinders of parallel axes; exact lines, circles and ellipses restricted to both boxes and cut at their seams, pcurves exact when linear, interpolated otherwise with their deviation. Other pairs are marked incomplete until the marching algorithm
* `gbs/intersect/curve_surface.h`: intersection of a curve and a surface on bounded ranges, giving every crossing and every portion of the curve lying on the surface. Closed forms for a line against the primitives (quadratic, quartic for the torus) and for a circle or an ellipse against a plane; otherwise subdivision of the curve against Bézier patches of B-spline surfaces (or sampled patches of other surfaces) and Gauss-Newton on (t, u, v). Tangent crossings are flagged (`gbs/intersect/surface_pieces.h`, `gbs/intersect/polynomial.h`)
* `gbs/intersect/curves.h`: intersection of two curves on bounded parameter ranges (any dimension), giving every crossing and every common portion: B-splines are cut into Bézier segments enclosed by the box of their poles and halved by de Casteljau, other curves are sampled; pairs of flat pieces are refined by Gauss-Newton. Tangent crossings are flagged and told apart from overlaps, closed curves are handled at their seam. First step of stage 2 of the BREP kernel (`docs/sources/design/brep_palier2.md`)
* `gbs/intersect/box.h`: `gbs::Box<T, dim>`, axis-aligned bounding box, and `segment_overlaps` (slab test)

Fixes
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h`: an intersection curve ending on the borders of the boxes could be missed: the start of a pair of flat patches was corrected with its parameters clamped to the boxes, and stalled on a border the step pushed it beyond. A parameter on a bound that the step pushes out is now held there and the others solved again (active set). `section_detail::VertexPool` skips the vertices merged away by an imprint
* `gbs/bscanalysis.h`: `max_curvature_pos` returned neither the position of the maximum curvature nor the curvature: it minimized 1 / |C''|^2 with NLopt and returned |C''|^2 at the point found (on an ellipse of radii 1 and 0.2, u = 0.434 and 1654 instead of the vertex and 25). It now returns the parameter and the value of the maximum of the curvature |C' x C''| / |C'|^3 (new `curvature(crv, u)`, exact in 2D and 3D), from samples on every knot span (8 per span, at least 64) refined by Brent (Boost.Math), and throws `std::invalid_argument` unless u1 < u2; `solver` is ignored (**breaking**: the second value is the curvature). `compute_rolling_ball` solves the equality of the radii along both normals by TOMS 748 (Boost.Math) instead of NLopt's COBYLA on the difference of their squares: centres exact to 1e-15 instead of 1e-6, 3 to 10 times faster; a jump of the difference across parallel normals is no longer taken for a root (centre at infinity between facing circles). **Breaking:** it throws `std::invalid_argument` for an unbounded crv2 (a `Line`: pass a segment) and `std::runtime_error` when no circle is tangent to both curves, instead of returning the closest approach as a solution. `closest_curve` / `closest_p_curve` did not compile once instantiated (`midPoint()`), and their comparator could throw inside a parallel `min_element`: they project the point globally on each curve in parallel (`transform_rethrow`) and return the nearest. `bscanalysis.h` no longer includes `nlopt.hpp`: NLopt no longer computes anything in gbs (second step of `docs/sources/design/nlopt_removal.md`)
* `gbs/extrema.h`: `extrema_curve_point`, `extrema_surf_pnt`, `extrema_curve_curve`, `extrema_curve_curve_lst` and `extrema_surf_curve` returned the square of the distance (the squared distance was squared again by `solve_D_nlop`). They now return the distance, so that the contact checks of `build_intersections` (thus `gordon`), of the TFI lattices (`gbs-mesh/tfi.h`) and the choice of the projection in `gbs-brep/pcurve.h` compare a distance to their tolerance (curves 1e-4 apart passed a check at 1e-6), and `dev_from_points` returns distances. **Breaking:** these checks are now as strict as their tolerance says, as the grid check of `gordon` already was (at `tol` = 1e-6, `build_intersections`, `lattice_intersections` and `tfi_mesh_2d` accepted curves up to 1e-3 apart, now up to 1e-6): pass a larger `tol` for curves that do not meet exactly. The extrema are computed by Newton's method instead of NLopt's COBYLA, with the second derivatives: a curve or a surface whose `value()` throws beyond the first derivatives must now override the new `has_second_derivatives()` to return false (as `brep::OffsetSurface` does), Gauss-Newton is then used (**breaking** for such user classes, which used to work): the `solver` parameter (Python `solver=`, which now warns) is ignored and will be removed with NLopt. The bracketed overloads start Newton from the two nearest samples (closed curves and surfaces), `extrema_surf_pnt` and `extrema_surf_curve` without a start throw `std::invalid_argument` on an unbounded surface, `extrema_curve_curve_lst` passes its `n_bracket_u` / `n_bracket_v`, and the gradient of the surface-curve problem (wrong arguments of the surface derivatives) is gone
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h`: a curve touching the boundary of a box where it leaves the other surface (the circle of a cap on the box of its plane disk, at the seam of the side) landed on that boundary, tangentially, and stopped a few 1e-6 short, so it was not closed: a landing where the curve only touches the bound (sine of its angle with the iso under `angular_tol`) is passed over for the next parameter to leave its box, started inside the boxes
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h`: a curve running along the border of a B-spline surface (the plane cap of a B-spline cylinder against its side) came out as a score of wrong branches: a predicted point beyond the bound by a rounding error (v = -3e-34) was taken as leaving the surface and returned without being corrected, so the marching accepted points 1e-2 off the intersection. Parameters beyond a bound by less than the rounding margin of the boxes (1e-10 (1 + |a| + |b|)) are put back on it before that test too, so that a point left beyond is out of the box and the marching lands; the boolean of a B-spline cylinder with its copy goes from 6.1 s to 2.3 s
* `gbs-brep/split.h`: a hole sharing edges with another loop (the outline of two regions touching along a line, around both of them) could be given to that loop, its first point lying on its boundary; the outer region then lost its hole, and a boolean of a box tangent to a cylinder could leave an open shell for a change of 1e-13 in a parameter. The point tested is now taken beside the hole, on the left of its polygon where the face owning it lies, half way to the nearest boundary in that direction (whatever the thickness of the face)
* `gbs/bscurve.h`, `gbs/intersect/curve_pieces.h`, `gbs/intersect/surface_pieces.h`: `bezier_poles`, the Bézier decomposition of a clamped B-spline by the algorithm A5.6 of The NURBS Book (all the interior knots raised to the degree at once, nullopt for a discontinuity), used for curves and, row by row then column by column, for surfaces instead of one knot insertion at a time, quadratic in the number of knots: the boolean of a rational B-spline cylinder with its copy goes from 63 s to 6 s
* `gbs/intersect/curves.h`, `gbs/intersect/curve_surface.h`: the projection of a point on a curve stopped on its nearest sample when Newton's denominator was negative (rational parametrizations), and on a closed curve or surface could start from the wrong end of the seam and stay against the bound (a point of a rational circle found 0.39 away from it); Gauss-Newton steps are used instead, from the two nearest samples
* `gbs-brep/coincident.h`: pcurves projected on B-spline surfaces are accepted within the approximation tolerance of the pcurves, the edge tolerance being raised to their deviation (the parametrization of a rational surface is only C1 at its knots); an edge crossing the seam of a closed B-spline face is split there
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h`: a curve running along the border of a B-spline surface failed at its ends: its interpolated pcurves went beyond the parametric rectangle (now kept inside it), parameters beyond the bounds by a rounding error were taken as leaving the surface (now put back on the bound), and the landing on a box chose a parameter out of its box only by a rounding error, with a negative ratio (now ignored); such curves were flagged singular, so that booleans of B-spline boxes failed
* `gbs-brep/classify.h`: parameters inside the rounding margin of the bounds were evaluated beyond them, which a B-spline surface refuses
* `gbs/intersect/surface_surface.h`: the pcurve of a closed-form curve ending at the apex of a cone (or at a pole) took there the angle of another generatrix and ran diagonally in (u, v); the angle at a singular sample is now extrapolated from its neighbours
* `gbs-brep/split.h`: a hole loop could be given to its twin, the outer loop made of the same edges (a rectangle cut in a plane, a point of its boundary found inside its own outline), leaving a face of zero area and making booleans fail ("a face without inside") for a box whose face contains the axis of a cylinder
* `gbs-brep/classify.h`: parameters a rounding error outside the bounds of the surface (0 against 2.4e-16 read from a file) were outside the face before the test of its boundary, so that an edge lying on a seam was not seen
* `gbs-brep/boolean.h`: the faces of the copies of the operands that the result does not keep stayed in the model (they were listed after their shells were erased), as did the edges and vertices of the attempt when an operation failed; edges and vertices created by an operation and not used are now erased
* `gbs-brep/section.h`: a small arc of a curve tangent to the boundary of a face was reported as an edge lying on the face; a piece near the boundary is now an edge of the boundary only where the curves overlap
* `gbs/intersect/marching.h`: a branch reduced to a point where the surfaces are not tangent (a start on both seams at once) was reported as a contact point, so that two OpenCascade cylinders crossing on their seams made the booleans refuse them; only tangent contacts are points now
* `gbs-brep/sweep.h`: a face with holes turned completely sweeps cavities: the solid is closed by the largest shell, the others are its voids
* `gbs-brep`: `bounding_box` of a face bounded by its wires only covered its edges, so a sphere bounded by its seam got the box of a half circle; the surface is now sampled over the box of the pcurves of every face. The filters of `section` and of the point/solid `classify` also grow their sampled boxes by 5 % of their size, and `classify` tests whether a point lies on a face before casting a ray (a ray starting on a surface could miss it by rounding)
* `gbs-brep`: a pcurve projected from an edge lying on the border of a surface could overshoot the parametric rectangle by rounding errors and make surface evaluations throw; its poles are now kept inside the rectangle
* `gbs-brep`: the global projection of a point on a surface (first sample of a projected pcurve) could stop in a local minimum on the opposite border of a closed surface for a point just before its seam; it now restarts Gauss-Newton from the nearest node of a grid when the first result is not on the surface
* `IgesWriter::add_geometry` (`gbs-io/iges.h`) wrote rational curves and surfaces with their homogeneous poles `(w x, w y, w z)` as control points, so circles, ellipses and rational surfaces were read back distorted, and 2D curves with a wrong coefficient stride; poles are now written as Cartesian points plus weights, 2D geometry with z = 0 (the NURBS writers of the BREP export moved to `iges.h` and are shared); checked by an OpenCascade round trip
* `add_dimension` of a rational curve or surface put the new coordinate in place of the weight (homogeneous poles `(w x, w y, w)` became `(w x, w y, w, val)`), giving a zero weight by default; the new coordinate is now inserted before the weight as `w val` (`add_dimension_homogeneous` in `gbs/transformpoints.h`); non-rational geometry is unchanged
* `Line::bounds()` returned `{numeric_limits<T>::min(), max()}`, i.e. a lower bound of ~2.2e-308 instead of an unbounded line: negative parameters were rejected by bounds checks and point projections were clamped at u ≈ 0. It now returns `{lowest(), max()}`; `solve_N_nlop` / `solve_D_nlop` pass such bounds to NLopt as ±∞ (unbounded variable), since a finite `max() - lowest()` overflows NLopt's initial step. The extrema overloads that bracket their initial guess by sampling the bounds (`extrema_curve_point`, `extrema_curve_curve`, `extrema_surf_curve` without u0) now throw `std::invalid_argument` on an unbounded range instead of sampling NaNs: pass an initial guess or trim the line

* Python: sub-module `gbs.brep` binding the native BREP core (model with typed identifiers, explorer and `TopologyIndex`, queries, `make_vertex` / `make_edge` / `make_wire` / `make_face` / `make_face_from_pcurves` / `sew` / `make_solid` / `make_compound` raising `gbs.brep.BRepError` on failure, `check`), and `IgesWriter.add_brep`; the gbs-brep headers are in the Doxygen documentation
* `arc_length_distrib_params`: curve parameters at prescribed normalized arc lengths, given as a list or as a law s(ξ) of the node index fraction ξ = i / (n − 1) (tanh, geometric… clustering in length on the original curve, without re-interpolating it); `uniform_distrib_params` is the law s(ξ) = ξ. Python bindings for both forms

Fixes
* MSVC builds no longer force `/fp:fast /arch:AVX2` (#109): `/fp:fast` broke the robust geometric predicates (`orient2d`) used by the Delaunay mesher, and `/arch:AVX2` made the binaries require an AVX2 CPU. v0.5.0 — 29 sep 2026

Behaviour changes
* loft: the skin-direction (v) parameters now default to the averaged chord-length of NURBS Book §10.3 instead of a uniform parametrization (#71) — lofted surfaces differ from 0.4.x for the same input
* `find_span` now follows NURBS Book A2.1 exactly (half-open convention `k[s] <= u < k[s+1]`, #42/#46): correct one-sided derivatives at knots of multiplicity > 1

Performance
* evaluation: allocation-free O(p²) evaluator and one-pass multi-order derivatives (#30); span reduction enabled for derivatives (~12x faster curve derivatives); per-call eval ~4x faster than OCCT
* bulk evaluators are parallel above a size threshold (#88, 12–30x over OCCT's scalar loop); grid sampling (`offset_points`, surface `discretize`) pre-sized and parallel (#89)
* Python: bulk evaluators return numpy arrays without copy (#97, ~10x at 1M points)
* interpolation: banded / sparse / separable solves (#34), then direct band assembly + no-pivot band LU (#96): ~10x on an 800-point build, now faster than OCCT and scipy at every tested size
* approximation: banded one-pass assembly and structured least-squares solve (#65, 2.5–9x vs OCCT)
* loft: v-system factorized once, batched pole solves (#40)
* new `build_batch` (`gbs/execution.h`): builds N independent interpolations/approximations in parallel, exceptions propagated to the caller (#91)
* mesh: `elliptic_structured_smoothing` sweeps rows in parallel, bit-identical to the serial loop (#90, 5x at 10k and 16x at 1M interior vertices on 64 cores)

New features
* degree reduction (NURBS Book A5.11, #78): `BSCurve::reduceDegree(tol)`, `BSSurface::reduceDegreeU/V(tol)` return (success, rigorous error bound) and leave the geometry untouched when not reducible within `tol`; Python bindings, plus `BSSurface.increaseDegreeU/V`
* hodograph (NURBS Book A3.3, #83): `derivative_curve(crv, k)` returns the k-th derivative of a non-rational curve as a `BSCurve` (C++ and Python)
* rational surface derivatives (NURBS Book A4.4, #33)
* spine-guided rational loft, and `loft_approx` (well-posed loft by approximation, #58/#63)
* vectorized derivatives w.r.t. curvilinear abscissa: curves `d_dms` / `d_dm2s`, surfaces `d_dmus` / `d_dmvs` / `d_dmu2s` / `d_dmv2s`
* Python bindings: `d_dm` / `d_dm2` (curves) and `d_dmu` / `d_dmv` / `d_dmu2` / `d_dmv2` (surfaces) accept a list of parameters
* `pygbs.gbs.__version__`; the installed CMake package now carries its version (`find_package(GBS 0.5)`)

Fixes
* `remove_knot` faithful to NURBS Book A5.8 for `num > 1` (#59)
* `refine_approx` stop criteria and pole-count guards (#65)
* rational `loft` with spine compiles and interpolates; rational `flat_v` overload returns a surface (#40/#58)
* hardened Bowyer-Watson cavity construction in the 2D Delaunay mesher (#48)
* gbs-occt: OpenCASCADE 8 support (7.x still supported), 2 runtime divergences fixed (#47)
* Python: `elliptic_structured_smoothing` ignored `n_it` and `tol` (a single sweep was done); `BSSurface.reverseV` reversed U; `vistaplot` ready for pyvista 0.50 (`copy(deep=...)`)

Build / tooling
